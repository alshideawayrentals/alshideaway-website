Fall Hiking Season Is Here: Your Guide to the State Natural Areas Near Us

By Gordon Cook · August 31, 2026

By Gordon Cook · August 31, 2026 · ← All posts

There's a shift happening out here, even if the thermometer hasn't quite caught up to it yet. The evenings have a little more give to them, the cicadas have gone quiet, and I've started seeing hiking boots by the door again instead of just flip-flops. September through November is prime trail season in the Hill Country, and we happen to sit within an easy drive of three of the best state natural areas in Texas. If you've been looking for a reason to get the family out on a trail this fall, here it is.

Lost Maples State Natural Area — Texas's Best Fall Color

About an hour west of us near Vanderpool, Lost Maples is the reason serious leaf-peepers plan their whole autumn around a Hill Country trip. The bigtooth maples along the Sabinal River turn brilliant shades of red, orange, and gold most years, usually peaking sometime in mid-to-late November once we get a good cold snap — it takes a real cold front, with nights dropping into the 40s, to trigger the best color, so the exact week shifts year to year. The park posts a Fall Foliage Report starting in late October if you want to time your visit close.

One thing worth knowing before you go: Lost Maples caps out at 250 vehicles on fall weekends and closes the gate once it's full. Regulars reserve their spot months ahead — some as early as February — through the Texas State Parks reservation system. If you've got your heart set on a fall Saturday there, don't wait until October to book it.

Hill Country State Natural Area — Practically Our Backyard

Closer to home, about 30 minutes down the road in Bandera, Hill Country State Natural Area is 40-some miles of rugged, undeveloped trail — no pavement, no crowds, just limestone hills, spring-fed creeks, and grassy valleys. It's open to hikers, mountain bikers, and horseback riders alike, and it's about as old-Texas as a park gets in this part of the state. Day use runs $6 a person (kids 12 and under free), and it fills up on nice weekends, so a reservation is worth making if you've got your heart set on a particular Saturday.

Albert & Bessie Kronkosky State Natural Area — Coming Soon, Six Minutes Down the Road

And then there's the one we're most excited about, mostly because it's practically in our front yard. The new Albert & Bessie Kronkosky State Natural Area is taking shape just down Highway 46, six minutes from our gate, and construction is moving right along — the nature center is up and trails are being cut. Texas Parks & Wildlife is still targeting a fall 2026 opening, though no firm date has been announced yet. We'll be the closest place to stay when those gates finally open, and we'll pass along the date the moment we hear it. (We wrote a whole post about what's coming to Kronkosky if you want the details.)

🌱 Fall Hiking Cheat Sheet

Lost Maples: ~1 hour away · $6/person (13+) · reserve early for Oct–Nov weekends · best color usually mid-to-late November
Hill Country State Natural Area: ~30 minutes away · $6/person (13+) · 40 miles of trail · hikers, bikers & horses welcome
Kronkosky State Natural Area: 6 minutes away · not yet open · targeting fall 2026
