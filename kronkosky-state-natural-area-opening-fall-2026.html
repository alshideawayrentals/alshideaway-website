Texas's Newest State Park Is Opening Six Minutes Down the Road

By Gordon Cook · August 30, 2026

By Gordon Cook · August 30, 2026 · ← All posts

This week at Al's Hideaway, Labor Day is bearing down on us fast — the Bandera Round-Up Market sets up at the county courthouse Saturday, September 5th, and if the last one was any indication, half of Bandera County will be there shopping local before the weekend's out. But the question I've been fielding all summer isn't about this weekend. It's about what's happening six minutes down Highway 46.

Texas's Newest State Natural Area Is Almost Here

The Texas Parks and Wildlife Department has been quietly building something special just up the road from us: the Albert and Bessie Kronkosky State Natural Area, 3,814 acres of rugged, untouched Hill Country at 7690 Highway 46, right here in Pipe Creek. It's set to open to the public this fall, and it's going to be one of the few places left where you can see what these hills looked like before anybody put up a fence.

We've had a front-row seat to the whole thing. We hosted the trail-building crew here at the Hideaway for a stretch while they worked, and hearing them talk about the project, you could tell it wasn't just a job to them — there's real pride going into every mile of trail out there.

What's Actually Going In

TPWD has laid out the plans, and it's a solid one for a natural area this size. When it opens, visitors will find:

  • About 25 miles of hiking trails, including front country, back country, and roughly 3 miles built fully accessible
  • Six cabins with electricity (no bathrooms — bring your flip-flops for the walk to the bathhouse)
  • Four screened shelters and 13 primitive tent sites, plus backcountry group and backpacking sites
  • A bathhouse with showers, an ADA-accessible cabin and campsites, and a headquarters building with a gift shop
  • An interpretive center with a nature center, amphitheater, and group pavilion for programs and events

Why It's Worth the Fuss

This isn't just another patch of ground with a parking lot. The land protects habitat for species you won't find just anywhere — the golden-cheeked warbler, the Texas spring salamander, the alligator lizard, all considered rare or threatened in this part of the state. TPWD has been running vegetation and wildlife surveys out there all year to make sure the trails go around the areas that matter most. It's a rare thing to get a brand-new state natural area built with that kind of care, and rarer still to have it land in our backyard.

So When Can You Actually Go?

As of now, TPWD is targeting fall 2026 — no firm calendar date yet, but construction is moving right along. Buildings are going up, roads are cut, and the turn lane off Highway 46 is already under construction to handle the traffic. We'll post the exact opening date here the minute it's announced, so keep an eye on this blog if you're planning a trip around it.

Make Al's Hideaway Your Basecamp

Here's the part I'm most excited about: when Kronkosky opens, Al's Hideaway will be the closest campground to it, period. Whether you end up in one of the park's own primitive sites or you'd rather have a hot shower and a real bed waiting for you at the end of a hike, we're six minutes away and ready now.

We've got 13 handcrafted log cabins, 10 full-hookup RV sites, and 9 cowboy campsites — pet-friendly with no pet fees, and no cleaning fees either. Book direct on our website and use promo code FUN for 10% off stays of 2 nights or more. You don't have to wait for Kronkosky to open to come enjoy the Hill Country — fall's about to bring cooler nights, clearer skies, and some of the best camping weather of the year.

We'll keep you posted as the opening gets closer. Until then, come on out and see us.
