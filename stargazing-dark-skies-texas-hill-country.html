Stargazing & Dark Skies in the Texas Hill Country

Real dark skies near San Antonio, kept dark on purpose for stargazers and wildlife.

← Texas Hill Country Guide

Out here, the sky is part of the stay. Al's Hideaway sits on 20 acres in Pipe Creek, between Bandera and Boerne, well past the glow of San Antonio. On a clear, moonless night you can see the Milky Way from your porch, a cowboy campsite or a lawn chair by your RV. You don't need to drive anywhere or bring any gear. Just step outside and look up.

We didn't end up with dark skies by accident. We built this place to keep them, and we protect them along with the birds and wildlife that depend on the dark.

Dark Skies, Kept Dark on Purpose

Plenty of places say they care about dark skies. A rule on paper doesn't make a sky dark, though. What makes it dark is what happens with the lights after sunset. Here's what we actually do:

  • Mostly solar outdoor lighting. Our outdoor lights are mainly solar and kept low. We chose that on purpose, so the night stays dark for stargazers and for the animals that live here.
  • Lighting that respects wildlife. Bright night lighting can confuse migrating birds and throw off the natural day-and-night rhythms of birds, insects and other animals. Keeping our lights low is how we look after them.
  • Only 3 of 20 acres developed. When we built Al's Hideaway, we had the land cleared only where the cabins, RV sites and roads needed to go. The other 17 acres are still native woods, so there's no sea of parking-lot lights around you.
  • No city glow. We're about 35 minutes from downtown San Antonio, far enough that the horizon glow fades and the stars take over.

We're not an officially designated dark-sky park, and we won't pretend to be. We just keep it dark, every night, and you can see the difference the minute you step outside.

Protecting the Wildlife That Shares Our Land

The same choices that protect the night sky protect the animals. Al's Hideaway is a Certified Wildlife Habitat through the National Wildlife Federation (habitat #352902). Here's what that looks like on the ground:

  • Native landscaping, kept on purpose. We kept the native plants and trees instead of clearing for lawns, so birds, butterflies and bees have food and cover all year.
  • Native bird, bee and butterfly gardens. We've already planted native gardens in front of MeMaw's Country Store and MeMaw's Cabin, and spread wildflower and bluebonnet seeds across the property. More gardens are coming along our trails and in front of the cabins through our Sponsor a Plot program, where people and local businesses can plant a garden and get a plaque with their name on it.
  • Room for the birds. We see golden-cheeked warblers, winter migrants, woodpeckers and more cardinals than we can count. Read about it in our guide to birding in Bandera County.
  • Dark nights for nocturnal animals. Owls, bats, deer and the rest of the night crowd do best when the dark is left alone.

What You Can See

  • The Milky Way. Its bright core is best from late spring through early fall, rising in the south after dark. In winter you get a fainter band overhead and some of the brightest stars of the year.
  • Meteor showers. The big ones are the Perseids in August and the Geminids in December, which peak the night of December 13–14, 2026.
  • Planets and constellations. Bright planets, Orion in the winter sky, and summer constellations like Scorpius low in the south.
  • Satellites and the space station. On a clear night, you'll usually spot a few satellites sliding across the sky.

Tips for Your Stargazing Night

  • Pick a dark week. The darkest nights fall in the week around the new moon. A bright moon washes out the faint stars.
  • Give your eyes 20 minutes. Put the phone away. Your eyes need time to adjust, and one look at a bright screen starts the clock over.
  • Use a red light. A red flashlight or the red-light setting on a headlamp lets you see your footing without ruining your night vision, or your neighbor's.
  • Bring a chair and a blanket. A reclining chair or a pad on the ground beats craning your neck. Hill Country nights can turn cool, even in spring and fall.
  • Plan two nights. Clouds can roll in on any night, so a second night gives you a backup.

Stargazing FAQ

Is Al's Hideaway a good place for stargazing near San Antonio?

Yes. We're in Pipe Creek, about 35 minutes from downtown San Antonio and 15 minutes from Bandera and Boerne, far from the city glow. Only 3 of our 20 acres are developed, and our outdoor lighting is mainly solar and kept low, so the night sky stays dark.

Can you see the Milky Way at Al's Hideaway?

On clear nights without much moon, yes. The bright core of the Milky Way is best from late spring through early fall.

Do I need a telescope?

No. The Milky Way, meteor showers and constellations are best with your own eyes. If you'd like one, we have telescopes guests can use for free. Just ask when you book.

Can I bring my dog?

Yes. Al's Hideaway is pet-friendly with no pet fees.

Is Al's Hideaway a certified wildlife habitat?

Yes. Al's Hideaway is a Certified Wildlife Habitat through the National Wildlife Federation (habitat #352902).
