Birding in Bandera County: 300+ Species, Winter Visitors & Where to Stay

By Gordon Cook · November 2, 2026

By Gordon Cook · November 2, 2026 · ← All posts

A butterfly feeding on a bloom in our native garden. The plants we're adding feed birds, bees and butterflies. Photo: Regina Pellegrin Photography

Folks come to the Hill Country for the rodeos and the river, but one of the best things out here doesn't cost a dime and doesn't need a ticket. It's the birds. Birders have logged more than 300 species in Bandera County (eBird's county checklist lists 326), and a good many of them pass right over our 20 acres in Pipe Creek. My granddaddy Al would sit still on a hillside, point out a cardinal or a hawk, and let the land do the talking. That's the spirit we're trying to keep going.

Why Bandera County Is a Birder's Place

We sit where the Edwards Plateau meets the brush country, with cedar breaks, live oaks, creek bottoms and limestone canyons all within a short drive. That mix of habitat is why the Texas Parks and Wildlife Department lists 21 wildlife viewing sites on its Bandera Loop of the Great Texas Wildlife Trail, including Bandera City Park, Hill Country State Natural Area and Lost Maples State Natural Area. The Bandera Loop page names golden-cheeked warblers, black-capped vireos, zone-tailed hawks, painted buntings, green kingfishers and summer tanagers, along with monarch butterflies and plenty of dragonflies. Many of those sites need advance contact and some are day-use only, so call ahead.

On our own land, only about 3 of the 20 acres are developed. The rest is native woods, two creeks and walking trails, home to woodpeckers, more cardinals than I can count, and the golden-cheeked warbler, an endangered songbird that nests only in Central Texas. Those warblers fly up from Central America, with the first arrivals showing up from late February into March. The males sing the most from March through May, so the best time to hear and see them is usually March into early May, and winter is a fine time to scout the trails and plan a spring trip.

Around here you can look for black-crested titmice, ladder-backed woodpeckers, Carolina and canyon wrens, scissor-tailed flycatchers, black-chinned hummingbirds, crested caracaras, wild turkeys and, in season, painted buntings. You can browse the full Bandera County list on eBird before you pack.

Winter Is Good Birding Weather

Don't put your binoculars away just because it's cool. Winter brings migrants down from the north, the leaves thin out so you can see into the trees, and the trails are quiet. A cabin porch with a cup of coffee and a pair of binoculars is hard to beat on a cold morning.

If you'd like a ranger-led walk, the Texas Parks and Wildlife birding calendar lists a Winter Bird Walk at Government Canyon State Natural Area on January 9, 2027 at 8 AM, a birdsong identification program on January 30 at 10 AM, and a Bird Walk on the backcountry trails on March 20 at 7:30 AM. Dates and times can change, and some programs fill up, so confirm with the park before you go. Remember that the January 30 weekend is also Cowboy Mardi Gras in Bandera, which has our cabins booked solid, so plan ahead.

Help Us Plant a Bird, Bee & Butterfly Haven

Here's where we could use your help. We're planting native, low-water gardens at the entrance and exit of our trails and out in the woods, with quiet spots where anyone can sit and watch. Native plants feed birds, butterflies and bees through Texas heat and drought, and give migrating birds a place to rest and refuel. It also supports Bandera County's effort to become a certified Bird City Texas community.

It's already paying off in a way we're proud of: Al's Hideaway is a Certified Wildlife Habitat through the National Wildlife Federation (habitat #352902). That's a nod to the woods, the creeks and the gardens we're growing for birds, bees and butterflies.

We don't have the budget to do it all at once, so we're inviting folks to sponsor a plot, one at a time:

  • Friend of the Garden, $25, goes toward native plants for the gardens.
  • Birdhouse or Feeder, $75, installed along our trails with an engraved name tag.
  • Trail Plot, $300, a native plant section along the trails with a 6x9 inch plaque and 15% off a stay of up to two nights.
  • Cabin Garden Plot, $350, a low-water native garden with a bird bath in front of a cabin porch, with a plaque and the same 15% off thank-you.

You can live anywhere and still take part, because we do the planting and watering. Many folks dedicate a plot to someone who loved the outdoors. It makes a mighty fine gift for the bird lover who has everything. Sponsorships aren't tax-deductible, since we're a family-owned business and not a nonprofit. Read all about sponsoring a plot here.

Your Birding Basecamp in Pipe Creek

Al's Hideaway is the closest campground to the new Albert and Bessie Kronkosky State Natural Area, and we're right in the middle of the Bandera Loop country. We have 13 handcrafted log cabins, 10 full-hookup RV sites and 9 cowboy campsites. We're pet-friendly with no pet fees and have no cleaning fees. Book direct and use promo code FUN for 10% off any stay of two nights or more. Bring the binoculars and a field guide, and we'll supply the porch.
