Between Christmas and New Year's: A Quiet Cabin Week in the Texas Hill Country

By Gordon Cook · November 23, 2026

By Gordon Cook · November 23, 2026 · ← All posts

The presents are opened, the leftovers are in the fridge, and the whole family is still off work and out of school. That strange, slow stretch between Christmas and New Year's is my favorite week of the year out here. This year Christmas lands on a Friday and New Year's Day is a Friday too, so there's a full week, Saturday, December 26 through Friday, January 1, to slow down and do very little on purpose.

Why Spend the Week in the Hill Country

By the 26th, most folks are worn out from driving, hosting and wrapping. A cabin in Pipe Creek is the opposite of all that. It's cool and quiet, there's a porch with a view of the oaks, and nobody expects you to cook for twelve. We sit between Bandera and Boerne, so you can drive to a town for a good meal or some sightseeing and then come home to dark country roads and a dark sky.

If you've got a crowd, that works too. Our covered pavilion and outdoor kitchen are open to all guests, and four of our one-bedroom cabins have full kitchens. Bring the cousins, the in-laws and the dog. We're pet-friendly with no pet fees, and there are no cleaning fees either.

What's Going On Nearby

This is a quiet week, and I'll be straight with you: many local events wrap up before Christmas. Here's what I've been able to confirm for the days that matter.

  • Christmas lights in Bandera. The lights at Bandera's City Park and the Courthouse are up through January 1, so you can still take a slow drive through town after dark.
  • New Year's Day, January 1. Bandera Cowboy Capital lists a First Day Hike and the Bandera Cowgirl Round Up for that day. Times and places hadn't been posted when I wrote this, so check their events page before you go. Texas state parks often host guided First Day Hikes, and the Hill Country State Natural Area, near Bandera, held one last January.
  • Town time. Bandera has plenty of places to eat and walk around, and Boerne's Main Street is always worth an afternoon. San Antonio's Riverwalk is an easy drive if you want the big-city lights.

Dates and times can change, so double-check anything before you load up the truck.

Do Nothing, Beautifully

Honestly, the best part of this week is the nothing. Sleep late. Drink your coffee on the porch. Walk our trails, since only about 3 of our 20 acres are developed and the rest is native woods and two creeks. On a clear night, step outside and look up. Our skies are dark, and winter air makes the stars look sharper. We have telescopes our guests can borrow, and they're free, if you'd like a closer look at the moon or a star cluster. You won't need one to enjoy it, though.

Ring in the New Year Country Style

New Year's Eve is a Friday this year. You can spend it in a crowd, or you can spend it on a porch under the stars with the people you like best. I know which one I'd pick. Stay the whole week, and you can start the new year with a hike or an easy morning, and you'll never have to fight New Year's traffic.

Where to Stay

We have 13 handcrafted log cabins, 10 full-hookup RV sites and 9 cowboy campsites. We're also the closest campground to the new Albert and Bessie Kronkosky State Natural Area, just down Highway 46. A longer stay is a good deal here: book direct and use promo code FUN for 10% off any stay of two nights or more. Holiday-week dates go fast, so if you have your eye on a cabin, don't wait.

Until next time…

Happy Trails.
Be Safe & God Bless.
— Al
